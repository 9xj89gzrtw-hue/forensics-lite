What this report cannot tell you

  • CEX wallets look like whales. A top holder may be a Binance/Coinbase omnibus wallet. This tool does not label addresses, so treat every large row as unverified.
  • balanceOf is current state, not history. Balances are read at the report's head block. A wallet that held 5% last week and dumped shows 0 here.
  • The log window can miss earlier transfers. Holders are discovered from Transfer logs inside the scanned range only. Wallets that received tokens before --from-block are invisible, which is why coverage of totalSupply is printed and often below 100%.
  • Pool/router detection is heuristic. We exclude pair addresses returned by DexScreener plus a short list of known routers. Undiscovered pools and fresh routers still appear as holders.
  • Fee-on-transfer and rebasing tokens distort shares. We read balances, not the amounts that moved, so reflections and burns are only visible through the balance delta.
  • Contract-held supply is not insider supply. Vesting contracts, treasuries, bridges and staking contracts are single addresses. High concentration can be legitimate lock-up.
  • LP burn is not LP safety. Burned LP with a mintable token, or a 100%-burned LP in a pool whose owner can still migrate it, is not protection. LP token ownership is only one signal.
  • Early buyers are not insiders. Anyone can be the first buyer in a block. No wallet clustering, no funding graph and no off-chain identity is used here.
  • Public RPC endpoints are rate limited. If a chunk failed, the window is incomplete. Re-run with a larger --chunk-size budget or a private endpoint and compare.
